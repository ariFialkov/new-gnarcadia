**Product.** Gnarcadia (Ontario corporation, Toronto) is a browser PWA arcade. Players fund an in-platform wallet, pay a cash entry fee into a lobby or match, compete in real time or in seeded asynchronous rounds against identical game states, and the prize pool less a disclosed platform fee ("rake") is paid by rank based solely on in-game performance. No house edge, no sweepstakes, no games of chance. Launch markets: Ontario, Alberta, British Columbia, Nova Scotia; California, Colorado, Missouri, New York, Ohio, Pennsylvania. Everywhere else geo-blocked. Controls: geolocation, KYC at first deposit/withdrawal, age gate, terms acceptance, responsible-play limits, double-entry ledger.

## 1. Executive summary

Every launch jurisdiction asks the same two questions. **First:** is a pay-to-enter, prize-pooled video game contest "gambling" (US) or a "game"/"lottery scheme"/"betting" (Canada), or a lawful skill contest? That depends on (a) the jurisdiction's chance/skill test and (b) whether *our specific games* satisfy it — a facts-and-evidence question counsel cannot answer without per-game skill analyses (§6). **Second:** is a cash wallet with deposits, winnings and withdrawals regulated stored value / money transmission / payment-service activity? A clean structure (licensed partner holds funds for players; no peer-to-peer transfers; withdrawals to source) matters as much as the gambling analysis.

**Bottom line.** We believe a pure-skill, peer-funded, raked video-game contest is lawful in all ten jurisdictions *if* our games are genuinely skill-determined under the strictest applicable tests (Canada's "systematic resort to chance"; NY/MO "material degree") and *if* the wallet avoids unlicensed money-transmission / PSP status. We need written opinions on both legs before launch, and a specific recommendation on whether to defer Pennsylvania.

## 2. United States framework

### 2.1 The tests

1. **Predominance / dominant factor** — gambling only if chance predominates (majority rule; CA, PA, OH).
2. **Material element** — gambling if chance plays a *material* role even though skill is also a factor (NY, MO). A skill-dominant game can still be a "contest of chance."
3. **Any chance** — gambling if any chance affects outcome (a few states; none of ours, but Canada's federal test is functionally close — §3).
4. **Express skill carve-out** from a broad "risking money on an outcome" definition (CO).

### 2.2 State by state

**California — predominance.** Lotteries (Pen. Code § 319: prize, consideration, chance), banked/percentage games (§ 330), slot machines (§ 330b, with an amusement exception for predominantly-skill devices paying only free plays) and unlicensed "controlled games" (§ 337j, [text](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-pen/part-1/title-9/chapter-10/section-337j)) are prohibited; courts apply the "dominant factor" test ([Cal Gaming Law](https://calgaminglaw.com/what-determines-if-a-game-is-one-of-skill-or-chance/); [AG device guidance](https://www.oag.ca.gov/sites/all/files/agweb/pdfs/gambling/ill_devices.pdf)). Enforcement: the AG and ten DA offices obtained a $3.5M UCL/false-advertising judgment against Pong Game Studios (a Canadian vendor) because its "skill" elements were too simplistic ([CA AG](https://www.oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/attorney-general-bonta-secures-multimillion-dollar-judgment-against-pong-game)). **AB 831** (signed Oct 11, 2025; eff. Jan 1, 2026) bans dual-currency sweepstakes casinos and extends liability to geolocation, payment and content vendors who knowingly support them ([ZwillGen](https://www.zwillgen.com/gaming/californias-ab-831-bans-sweepstakes-casinos-expands-liability-vendors/)); it does not target skill contests but our vendors will diligence us against it. B&P § 17539.1 imposes detailed disclosures on contests requiring payment to enter or remain (anticipated contestants, solve rates, maximum cost to win) ([text](https://california.public.law/codes/business_and_professions_code_section_17539.1)); drafted for puzzle contests, **its application to video-game contests is open.**

**Colorado — skill carve-out.** "Gambling" is risking money for gain contingent "in whole or in part upon lot, chance, the operation of a gambling device, or the happening or outcome of an event … over which the person taking a risk has no control," but **does not include** "bona fide contests of skill, speed, strength, or endurance in which awards are made only to entrants or the owners of entries" (C.R.S. § 18-10-102(2), [text](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_18-10-102)). Colorado registers fantasy-contest operators (Division of Gaming, since 2017, 18+) and treats them as skill ([Division of Gaming](https://sbg.colorado.gov/gaming/fantasy-sports)). Physical skill terminals remain contested: a 2025 court ordered seized machines returned to operator Trey Franzoy as skill-based and he has sued the Division and three cities ([KRDO](https://krdo.com/news/2025/12/19/skilled-gaming-or-illegal-gambling-colorado-springs-pueblo-facing-lawsuit-at-the-heart-of-it/)). Confirm the rake is a fee, not an "award," under the carve-out.

**Missouri — material degree.** "Contest of chance" means any contest, game, gaming scheme or device "in which the outcome depends in a material degree upon an element of chance, notwithstanding that the skill of the contestants may also be a factor" (§ 572.010, [FindLaw](https://codes.findlaw.com/mo/title-xxxviii-crimes-and-punishment-peace-officers-and-public-defenders/mo-rev-st-572-010/)). In *TNT Amusements v. Torch Electronics* (E.D. Mo., Ross J., declaratory judgment Feb 13, 2026, after an Oct 2025 jury verdict) "no-chance" devices were gambling devices because shuffled multiplier pools and RNGs made outcomes depend in a material degree on chance ([Casino.org](https://www.casino.org/news/federal-judge-rules-missouri-gray-market-gambling-machines-illegal/); [Missouri Independent](https://missouriindependent.com/2026/04/08/missouri-company-behind-gas-station-slot-machines-suspending-operations-amid-criminal-probe/)). Skillz, Papaya and MPL all offer cash play in Missouri; treat it nonetheless as a strict state for RNG design.

**New York — material degree (Penal Law) vs dominance (Constitution).** Penal Law § 225.00(1) mirrors Missouri's "material degree" wording. *White v. Cuomo* (Ct. App., Mar 22, 2022) held interactive fantasy sports are not constitutional "gambling" (skill dominates) while noting the Penal Law's threshold is lower ([opinion](https://nycourts.gov/REPORTER/3dseries/2022/2022_01954.htm); [Foley](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2022/04/a-potential-new-paradigm-for-daily-fantasy-sports/)). **S5935A** (signed Dec 5, 2025, immediate) bans "online sweepstakes games" — dual currency *and* casino-style simulation — and bars payment processors, geolocation providers and content vendors from supporting them, fines to $100,000 ([bill PDF](https://legislation.nysenate.gov/pdf/bills/2025/S5935A); [SBC Americas](https://sbcamericas.com/2025/12/08/new-york-hochul-signs-sweepstakes-ban/)). Skill contests are outside the definition; the vendor-liability template is now in regulators' hands.

**Ohio — predominance with a "pool" wrinkle.** "Game of chance" is a game "in which a player gives anything of value in the hope of gain, the outcome of which is determined largely by chance"; "scheme of chance" includes a slot machine, lottery, numbers game, **"pool conducted for profit,"** or other consideration-for-a-chance scheme, excluding bingo, skill-based amusement machines and non-profit pools (R.C. § 2915.01; prohibition § 2915.02, [Justia](https://law.justia.com/codes/ohio/title-29/chapter-2915/section-2915-02/); [ch. 2915](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/chapter-2915)). The "skill-based amusement machine" rules (no chance element; $10 prize cap, [OAC 3772-50-24](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/ohio/Ohio-Admin-Code-3772-50-24)) are device-specific. AG Opinions 2004-029 and 2006-046 address skill devices (contents not independently verified).

**Pennsylvania — predominance overlaid by the June 2026 ruling.** 18 Pa.C.S. § 5513 prohibits gambling devices and unlawful gambling; *Commonwealth v. Dent*, 992 A.2d 190 (Pa. Super. 2010) supplies the predominance test ([summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_v._Dent)). On **June 15, 2026**, in *In re: Three Pennsylvania Skill Amusement Devices* (No. 50 MAP 2024, consolidated with *POM of Pennsylvania v. Department of Revenue*), the Supreme Court held 4–2 that skill-game *devices* are "slot machines" under the Gaming Act (4 Pa.C.S. § 1103, as amended in 2017 to include "skill slot machines" and "hybrid slot machines") and unlawful when unlicensed under the Gaming Act and § 5513, with a 120-day stay ([Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/pennsylvania/supreme-court/2026/50-map-2024.html); [Saiber](https://www.saiber.com/insights/publications/2026-06-16-pennsylvania-supreme-court-rules-skill-game-devices-are-slot-machines); [Barley Snyder](https://www.barley.com/pennsylvania-supreme-court-rules-skill-games-are-slot-machines-what-businesses-need-to-know/); [Yogonet](https://www.yogonet.com/international/news/2026/06/16/124099-pennsylvania-supreme-court-rules-skill-games-are-slot-machines-under-state-law)). **Online reach is unsettled.** No app or online tournament was before the Court; the holding rests on a device definition ("mechanical, electrical or computerized contrivance" into which consideration is paid) applied to terminals in bars and stores ([Lower Bucks Times](https://lowerbuckstimes.com/2026/07/14/pennsylvania-skill-games-supreme-court-clock/); [Spotlight PA](https://www.spotlightpa.org/news/2026/06/skill-games-illegal-pennsylvania-supreme-court-ruling-capitol/)). Skillz, Papaya and MPL still list PA as cash-eligible. Counsel must opine whether a browser game on the player's own device is a "slot machine"/"gambling device," whether *Dent* still governs non-device contests, and whether the Gaming Act's "interactive gaming" definitions capture peer-to-peer skill contests. Legislature: HB 2801/2802 (Rep. Diamond) would tax and regulate terminals; the 2026-27 budget was silent; the stay expires around Oct 13–15, 2026 ([Yogonet](https://www.yogonet.com/international/news/2026/09/02/126195-pennsylvania-lawmaker-proposes-legalising-and-taxing-skill-games); [PA House GOP](https://www.pahousegop.com/News/38829/Latest-News/Pair-of-Bills-Would-Finally-Address-Skill-Games-in-Statute--Diamond-Says); [Capital-Star](https://penncapital-star.com/justice-the-courts/pa-lawmakers-renew-push-to-regulate-tax-skill-games-after-high-court-ruling/)).

### 2.4 Federal overlay

- **UIGEA (31 U.S.C. §§ 5361–5367).** Bars gambling businesses from accepting payments for "unlawful Internet gambling," defined by reference to *state* law where the bet is made. "Bet or wager" excludes games where participants risk only personal effort and carves out qualifying fantasy sports and educational contests ([§ 5362](https://us.vlex.com/vid/definitions-671119745)). "Game subject to chance" is undefined; the Third Circuit rejected a vagueness challenge in *iMEGA v. Attorney General* (2009) ([LJN](https://www.lawjournalnewsletters.com/2009/09/29/third-circuit-upholds-online-gambling-ban)). Regulation GG (12 CFR Part 233) makes banks and networks police "restricted transactions," so processors underwrite off state-law legality ([Treasury rule](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/136/archive-documents/unlawfuinternetgambling11.12.08.pdf)). A clean state-by-state skill opinion is a commercial necessity.
- **Wire Act (18 U.S.C. § 1084).** Limited to sports betting after *NH Lottery Comm'n v. Rosen* (1st Cir. 2021); DOJ did not seek certiorari ([opinion](https://www.ca1.uscourts.gov/sites/ca1/files/opnfiles/19-1835P-01A.pdf); [BHFS](https://www.bhfs.com/insight/doj-throws-in-the-towel-on-wire-act-interpretation-but-congressional-action-may-loom/)). Low relevance.
- **Illegal Gambling Business Act (18 U.S.C. § 1955).** Federalises a state-law gambling violation involving five or more persons operating 30+ days or grossing $2,000/day; no independent definition of gambling — the enforcement multiplier if a state analysis fails.
- **FTC Act § 5 / state UDAP.** The FTC's $520M Epic Games action (dark patterns, COPPA) is the reference for purchase flows and minors ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/node/80648)); state analogues (Cal. UCL/FAL used against Pong; N.Y. GBL § 349).
- **Sweepstakes/promotion law.** Irrelevant to the core product, but any giveaway (bonus credits, referral prizes) must follow state sweepstakes rules (no-purchase-necessary, official rules, FL/NY/RI registration above thresholds). Keep promotions separate from contests.
- **Unclaimed property.** Dormant balances become reportable after 3–5 years (New Jersey: internet-gaming accounts dormant 3 years) ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/kpmg-us/content/dam/kpmg/pdf/2024/online-gaming-unclaimed-property-duties.pdf); [Aprio](https://www.aprio.com/insights-events/unclaimed-property-obligations-in-the-gaming-industry-ins-article-tech/)).

## 3. Canada framework

### 3.1 Criminal Code

Gaming is federal criminal law. Section 197 defines "game" as "a game of chance or mixed chance and skill." Section 206 criminalises lottery schemes, including (s. 206(1)(f)) disposing of goods "by any game of chance or any game of mixed chance and skill in which the contestant or competitor pays money or other valuable consideration" ([s. 206](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-46/section-206.html)). Section 207 permits "lottery schemes" conducted and managed by a province (s. 207(1)(a)) or its licensees. Sections 201–202 cover gaming/betting houses, bookmaking and pool-selling ([s. 202](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/c-46/section-202.html)). **A game of pure skill is not a "game"** and falls outside ss. 201, 206 and 207 ([Lexology intro](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=c86037c4-37a3-4174-adc2-2c3a8afbc9ec); [Segev In-Depth](https://segevllp.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Lexology-In-Depth-Gambling-Law-Canada.pdf)).

### 3.2 "Systematic resort to chance" — stricter than US predominance

*Ross, Banks and Dyson v. The Queen*, [1968] S.C.R. 786: a game is mixed chance and skill only where there is a "systematic resort to chance" to determine outcomes, as opposed to "the unpredictables that may occasionally defeat skill" ([IAGR](https://iagr.org/industry-news/legal-update-games-of-skill/); [Canadian Lawyer](https://www.canadianlawyermag.com/cl-plus/cases/11780)). Canada does **not** apply predominance: design that systematically injects chance into outcome makes the game "mixed" however skill-dominant ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/canada/gaming/1175862/ontario-igaming-registration-requirements-and-agco-updates)). Key cases:

- *R. v. Balance Group International Trading* (Ont., c. 2002; citation not verified) — claw crane: player control so minimal that the game was chance or at best mixed, "an overwhelming degree of chance and merely a dash of skill" ([IAGR](https://iagr.org/industry-news/legal-update-games-of-skill/)).
- *AGCO v. SBG-Skill Based Games* (GotSkill), 2019 ONCA 648 (citation per secondary sources; verify): mixed chance and skill because randomness of subsequent challenges satisfied the chance element even though an exceptional player could beat it ([Gilbertson Davis](https://www.gilbertsondavis.com/?p=23240); [IMGL](https://www.imgl.org/publications/imgl-magazine-volume-3-no-1/why-some-slots-are-not-regulated-gambling-products-in-ontario/)). *SBG-Skill v. Registrar, Alcohol and Gaming*, 2025 ONSC 4938 (Nakatsuru J., Aug 28, 2025): the redesign still had "a systematic resort to chance in how the game incentivizes the players to spend money in the hopes of uncovering a valuable round in the future" ([Canadian Gaming Business](https://www.canadiangamingbusiness.com/2025/09/04/ontario-judge-gotskill-agco-decision/); [case PDF](https://www.canadianlawyermag.com/cl-plus/cases/download/11780/8218/SBG-Skill%20v.%20Registrar,%20Alcohol%20and%20Gaming,%202025%20ONSC%204938.pdf)). AGCO then removed GotSkill and "Prime Slots" machines province-wide and revoked retailer registrations ([AGCO](https://www.agco.ca/en/news/agco-requires-liquor-licensed-establishments-ontario-stop-offering-gotskill-gambling-machines); [CasinoBeats](https://casinobeats.com/2025/09/29/ontario-agco-skill-game-machines-crackdown/)).
- *R. v. Riesberry*, 2015 SCC 65 (breadth of "game") ([SCC](https://decisions.scc-csc.ca/scc-csc/scc-csc/en/item/15682/index.do)).

**Design implications.** (1) *Seeded randomness*: all competitors in a match get the identical seed; counsel should still opine whether identical seeds satisfy *Ross* when the GotSkill courts looked at how chance drives *spending incentives*, not only head-to-head fairness. (2) *Matchmaking luck*: disclosed skill-based matchmaking and leaderboard formats (many players, one seeded round) reduce the "who you draw" argument. (3) *Random power-ups / loot / critical hits*: any in-match RNG affecting score that is not identical for all competitors must go. (4) *Variable prize mechanics* (mystery multipliers, surprise bonus rounds, random jackpots) are exactly what sank GotSkill and Torch; prizes must be a fixed, pre-disclosed function of rank.

### 3.3 Lottery scheme or betting?

If the game is pure skill, ss. 206–207 do not apply. The residual risk is **s. 202 (pool-selling / business of betting)**: a platform taking a fee from a pool could be characterised as pool-selling if the entry fee is viewed as a *bet on an outcome* rather than consideration for *participation*. Commentary suggests informal pools may be tolerated but an organiser taking a fee is at risk ([Lexology intro](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=c86037c4-37a3-4174-adc2-2c3a8afbc9ec)). We need counsel to confirm the tournament/wager distinction (participants competing *for* a prize funded by entry fees versus persons wagering *on* an event they do not control). **Unsettled — the most important Canadian opinion item.**

### 3.4 Provincial overlays

- **Ontario.** AGCO regulates under the *Gaming Control Act, 1992*; iGaming Ontario conducts and manages private-operator igaming under s. 207(1)(a); operators register with AGCO (CAD $100,000/site/year) and follow the Registrar's Standards, which treat esports as a "sport" and peer-to-peer games as lottery schemes ([AGCO roles](https://www.agco.ca/en/lottery-and-gaming/roles-agco-and-igaming-ontario); [Standards](https://www.agco.ca/en/book/export/html/245361); [registration](https://www.agco.ca/en/lottery-and-gaming/apply-igaming-operator-registration)). The Court of Appeal's Nov 2025 reference confirms peer-to-peer games of chance/mixed chance and skill are lawful *only* within the iGO model ([BLG](https://www.blg.com/en/insights/2025/11/ontario-court-of-appeal-decision-allows-international-pooled-liquidity-in-online-gaming); [Cassels](https://cassels.com/insights/reference-re-igaming-ontario-onca-permits-pooled-liquidity-scheme/)). **iGO registration is neither required nor available for a pure-skill contest**; if our games are "mixed," the only lawful route is to become (or supply) an iGO operator. Counsel should advise whether a courtesy AGCO consultation is prudent. The *Consumer Protection Act, 2002* applies: ss. 7–8 void pre-dispute arbitration clauses and class waivers; O. Reg. 17/05 governs internet agreements (disclosure, copy within 15 days, cancellation) and gift-card expiry/fees ([O. Reg. 17/05](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/050017/v15); [Mann Lawyers](https://www.mannlawyers.com/resources/internet-agreements-with-ontario-consumers-consumer-protection-act-considerations-for-businesses/)). The *Consumer Protection Act, 2023* (royal assent Dec 6, 2023) **was still not proclaimed as of mid-2026**; regulations were consulted through 2025 ([Osler](https://www.osler.com/en/insights/updates/incoming-changes-to-consumer-protection-laws-implications-for-financial-service-providers/); [Cassels](https://cassels.com/insights/bill-142-ontarios-new-consumer-protection-legislation-has-passed-heres-how-you-can-impact-the-development-of-corresponding-regulations/)) — confirm at engagement.
- **Alberta.** AGLC under the *Gaming, Liquor and Cannabis Act*; the *iGaming Alberta Act* (Bill 48, royal assent May 12, 2025) created the Alberta iGaming Corporation; registration opened Jan 13, 2026; market launched July 13, 2026 ([Gowling](https://gowlingwlg.com/en/insights-resources/articles/2026/entering-alberta-igaming-market-registration-roadmap-for-operators); [GBG](https://www.gbg.com/en/blog/what-s-the-game-plan-for-igaming-alberta/)). Mixed games must be inside the model; pure skill is outside. Gambling age 18.
- **British Columbia.** BCLC conducts all gambling (PlayNow only); regulator GPEB replaced by the Independent Gambling Control Office in April 2026 (reported; verify) ([Casino.org BC](https://www.casino.org/canada/british-columbia/)). No private licence; a "mixed" finding is fatal. Age 19.
- **Nova Scotia.** Atlantic Lottery Corporation exclusively; Service NS Alcohol, Gaming, Fuel and Tobacco Division administers the *Gaming Control Act* ([NS](https://novascotia.ca/sns/access/alcohol-gaming/about/acts-regulations-and-policies.asp); [CGB](https://www.canadiangamingbusiness.com/?p=12882)). No private route. Age 19.
- **Quebec (excluded).** Quebec repealed its publicity-contest regime (Régie filings, duties, Quebec-specific rules) effective Oct 27, 2023 (Bill 17) ([BLG](https://www.blg.com/en/insights/2023/11/quebec-abolishes-publicity-contest-rules); [Gowling](https://gowlingwlg.com/en-ca/insights-resources/articles/2023/game-changer-quebec-abolishes-contest-laws)), so contest rules are no longer the reason. Remaining reasons: *Charter of the French Language* as amended by Bill 96 (French-first UI, contracts, support, marketing), Quebec's stricter *Consumer Protection Act*, Law 25 privacy (full force Sept 2024, [OneTrust](https://OneTrust.com/blog/quebecs-bill-64-adopted/)), and Loto-Québec's monopoly if any game is "mixed."

### 3.5 Competition Act s. 74.06

Any "contest, lottery, game of chance or skill, or mixed chance and skill" requires "adequate and fair disclosure" of the number and approximate value of prizes, regional allocation and any fact materially affecting the chances of winning, conspicuously and without inconveniencing entrants, with prizes distributed without undue delay ([s. 74.06](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-34/section-74.06.html); [Bureau guidelines](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/promotional-contests-enforcement-guidelines); [Miller Thomson](https://www.millerthomson.com/en/insights/marketing-advertising-and-product-compliance/dont-let-your-contest-become-a-compliance-risk-a-practical-guide-for-businesses/)). For us: pool, rake, payout table and material facts (matchmaking, no bots, seeding) shown *before* the fee is taken.

## 4. Money and payments law

### 4.1 US federal (FinCEN)

Accepting funds from one person and transmitting them to another is money transmission (31 CFR 1010.100(ff)(5)); moving losers' entry fees to winners is, in form, exactly that. Skillz's 10-K concedes money-transmission, banking and unclaimed-property laws "may be interpreted to cover" platform contests and entry fees ([Skillz FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1801661/000180166126000020/sklz-20251231.htm)). "Prepaid access" is access to funds paid in advance via an electronic device or vehicle (31 CFR 1010.100(ww)); **closed-loop** access usable only with a defined merchant is excluded from "prepaid program" status at ≤ $2,000/day ([31 CFR 1010.100](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.100); [FinCEN FAQs](https://www.fincen.gov/resources/statutes-regulations/guidance/frequently-asked-questions-regarding-prepaid-access)). A wallet that pays out *cash* and allows withdrawal is hard to call closed-loop.

**Structures that reduce exposure (for counsel to confirm):** (i) a licensed partner (bank or licensed MSB/program manager) holds all player funds in FBO accounts and is the transmitter, Gnarcadia being program manager; (ii) **no peer-to-peer transfers**; (iii) withdrawals only to the original funding instrument/name; (iv) instant settlement so Gnarcadia never holds a float in its own name; (v) daily balance and deposit caps; (vi) **agent-of-payee** positioning for the entry fee (weakened because the winner, not Gnarcadia, is the ultimate recipient). Regardless of status, five-year BSA retention (31 CFR 1010.430, [text](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.430)) and processor Reg GG diligence apply in practice.

### 4.2 State money transmitter laws

Thirty-one states had enacted the CSBS Model Money Transmission Modernization Act (MTMA) by early 2026; full adoptions include a standard agent-of-payee exemption (§ 3.01(b)) ([Stinson](https://www.stinson.com/newsroom-publications-massachusetts-adopts-the-model-money-transmission-modernization-act-joining-25-other-states); [Alston & Bird](https://www.alston.com/en/insights/publications/2025/01/model-money-transmission-modernization-act); [CSBS](https://www.csbs.org/mtma-legislative-update-4232026)).

| State | Statute / regulator | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| CA | Money Transmission Act, Fin. Code § 2000 et seq.; DFPI; MTMA-aligned | "Stored value" excludes closed-loop; agent-of-payee exemption (§ 2010). Recommendation to test with counsel: do not pursue six MTLs at launch; use a licensed partner holding funds FBO players and obtain written analysis (plus interpretive letters where available) that Gnarcadia is not itself a transmitter.

### 4.3 Canada — FINTRAC (PCMLTFA)

An MSB is a person in the business of foreign exchange dealing, "remitting funds or transmitting funds by any means," issuing/redeeming money orders, dealing in virtual currency or crowdfunding ([FINTRAC](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/msb-esm/msb-eng); [registration check](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/msb-esm/questions/2-eng)). In April 2022 FINTRAC retracted PI-7670, so merchant settlement and payment processing can be MSB activities ([Osler](https://www.osler.com/en/insights/updates/fintrac-retracts-merchant-servicing-and-payment-processing-exemptions/); [Goodmans](https://www.goodmans.ca/insights/article/fintrac-retracts-pi-7670-certain-merchant-servicing-and-payment-processing-activities-may-be-msb-activities); [FINTRAC notice](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/notices-avis/2022-07-21-eng)). Questions: is moving entry fees from losers to winners "remitting or transmitting funds"? does accepting USD and paying CAD make us a foreign-exchange dealer? is a redeemable closed-loop wallet distinguishable? FINTRAC's casino obligations apply only to provincially authorised casinos. Either way we will run MSB-grade KYC, five-year records and suspicious-transaction escalation because our bank will require it ([FINTRAC MSB records](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/guidance-directives/recordkeeping-document/record/msb-eng)).

### 4.4 Canada — Retail Payment Activities Act

PSPs must register with the Bank of Canada before performing retail payment activities; registration opened Nov 1–15, 2024 and operational-risk and **safeguarding-of-funds** obligations took effect Sept 8, 2025 ([Bank of Canada FAQ, Jan 2026](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/2026/01/frequently-asked-questions-about-retail-payments-supervision/); [criteria](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/2024/10/criteria-for-registering-payment-service-providers/); [Baker McKenzie](https://www.globalcompliancenews.com/2024/11/26/https-insightplus-bakermckenzie-com-bm-financial-institutions_1-canada-deadline-approaching-for-registration-under-the-retail-payment-activities-act/)). Payment functions include "provision or maintenance of an account held on behalf of one or more end users" and "**holding of funds on behalf of an end user**" ([RPAA](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/R-7.36/page-1.html); [Bennett Jones](https://www.bennettjones.com/Insights/Blogs/Retail-Payment-Activities-Act-A-Deeper-Dive-Into-When-Registration-is-Required)). Exclusions: s. 6 closed-loop merchant instruments (gift cards) and activities "incidental" to a non-payment business ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-us/insights/publications/2025/01/breaking-down-exceptions-and-exclusions-in-the-retail-payment-activities-act); [Regulations](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p2/2023/2023-11-22/html/sor-dors229-eng.html)). A withdrawable CAD wallet looks like "holding funds on behalf of an end user" and is not gift-card closed-loop; "incidental" is our best argument but untested for gaming wallets. **If in scope, registration and trust/insurance safeguarding follow** — another reason to have a registered PSP/bank hold the funds.

### 4.5 Provincial stored-balance rules

O. Reg. 17/05 prohibits expiry dates on gift-card agreements and restricts value-reducing fees; comparable rules exist elsewhere ([O. Reg. 17/05](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/050017/v15); [BLG](https://www.blg.com/en/insights/2018/01/ontario-government-proposes-to-clarify-gift-card-rules-under-the-consumer-protection-act)). Whether a cash balance is a "gift card" is debatable; the safe design is no expiry, no dormancy fees, no forfeiture, and a documented return process.

### 4.6 Tax

**US reporting.** Under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act the Form 1099-MISC (box 3 prizes) and 1099-NEC thresholds rise from $600 to **$2,000 for payments in 2026**, indexed from 2027; 24% backup withholding still applies where a payee fails to furnish a valid TIN ([TaxBandits](https://blog.taxbandits.com/form-1099-misc-instructions-for-2026-tax-year-a-complete-guide/); [Patriot](https://www.patriotsoftware.com/blog/accounting/1099-reporting-threshold/); [CPA Practice Advisor](https://cpapracticeadvisor.com/2025/07/30/one-big-beautiful-bill-act-changes-1099-thresholds/165863/)). Collect **W-9** from US players at first withdrawal and **W-8BEN** from non-US players; confirm with tax counsel that a Canadian payor making US-source prize payments has 1099/backup-withholding duties (we assume yes; **verify**), whether to report gross or net of entry fees, and state withholding (none known in our six states; **verify** PA/NY).

**Canada — players.** Casual winnings are generally non-taxable windfalls (Folio S3-F9-C1, [CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/technical-information/income-tax/income-tax-folios-index/series-3-property-investments-savings-plans/series-3-property-investments-savings-plans-folio-9-miscellaneous-payments-receipts/income-tax-folio-s3-f9-c1-lottery-winnings-miscellaneous-receipts-income-losses-crime.html)). Caveats: ITA s. 56(1)(n) taxes "a prize for achievement in a field of endeavour ordinarily carried on by the taxpayer" above $500, and CRA has indicated payers may have T4A obligations under Reg. 200(2) ([CRA TI 2014-0539611E5](https://members.videotax.com/technical-interpretations/2014-0539611E5-t4a-slips-for-prizes-and-awards-from-contests)); full-time players may be carrying on a business (professional-poker cases, [Canadian Accountant](https://canadian-accountant.com/content/practice/poker-winnings-tax-court-decisions)). Ask whether video-game prizes to casual players are windfalls or 56(1)(n) prizes and whether T4As are required.

**GST/HST.** Rake and cosmetics are taxable supplies; for Canadian consumers HST/GST applies at the player's province under place-of-supply rules; supplies to non-residents are generally zero-rated (**verify**) ([CRA Notice 322](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/notice322/recovery-gst-hst-under-digital-economy-measures.html)). Confirm whether the *entry fee itself* is consideration for a taxable supply or a pass-through to a prize pool held in trust — a ~13% pricing question.

**US sales tax on digital goods.** Colorado taxes digital goods since July 1, 2021 (HB21-1312, [Sales Tax Institute](https://www.salestaxinstitute.com/resources/colorado-enacts-new-legislation-codifying-treatment-of-digital-goods-as-tangible-personal-property)); Ohio taxes specified digital products; Pennsylvania taxes digital products; New York does not tax digital goods generally but taxes prewritten software/SaaS ([NY advisory](https://www.tax.ny.gov/pdf/advisory_opinions/sales/a11_20s.pdf)); Missouri generally does not ([Numeral](https://www.numeral.com/blog/saas-sales-tax-missouri)); California does not, though a 2027 SaaS change is reported ([vatcalc](https://www.vatcalc.com/?p=52212); [Dodo guide](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/sales-tax-digital-goods-by-state)). Classification of cosmetics and platform fees is state-specific; use a tax engine and get a taxability matrix.

## 5. Consumer-facing requirements

Clauses counsel should draft or validate:

1. **Nature of service / no-chance declaration** — contests are skill competitions among players; outcomes depend solely on performance; Gnarcadia is not a party to any wager and does not play against users. Must be provably true (*Skillz v. Papaya*).
2. **Official contest rules** per game: fee, rake, prize table, tie-breaks, seeding/matchmaking, disconnect handling, dispute window; s. 74.06 and (if applicable) B&P § 17539.1 disclosures.
3. **Eligibility** — age by jurisdiction (§3.6), physical presence in a permitted jurisdiction at entry, one account per person, exclusion of staff/family/self-excluded persons and of developers in their own titles.
4. **Void where prohibited; no VPN** — right to void contests and withhold prizes where location is unverified.
5. **Fair play / anti-bot / anti-collusion** — prohibit automation, multi-accounting, collusion, exploits; disclose that no house bots are used in cash play (or exactly when and how labelled); monitoring and appeals.
6. **Wallet** — funds held by [licensed partner] for players; no interest; no expiry or dormancy fees; withdrawal to funding source after verification; dormant-account return/escheat procedure.
7. **Refunds** — entry fees refunded only on cancelled/voided contests; unused deposits refundable to source; Ontario internet-agreement cancellation rights.
8. **KYC/AML consent** — identity verification, sanctions screening, source-of-funds, monitoring and reporting.
9. **Responsible play** — deposit/loss/time limits, cooling-off, self-exclusion (cross-game, irrevocable for its term), help lines by jurisdiction.
10. **Dispute resolution** — US: FAA arbitration with class waiver, with California drafting (McGill public-injunction rule, mass-arbitration fees). **Ontario: CPA 2002 ss. 7–8 invalidate pre-dispute arbitration clauses and class waivers against consumers**, and *Uber v. Heller*, 2020 SCC 16 adds unconscionability ([Bennett Jones](https://www.bennettjones.com/Insights/Articles/SCCs-Decision-in-the-Uber-Class-Action); [Osler on *Wellman*](https://www.osler.com/en/blogs/classactions/june-2017/wellman-v-telus-ontario-court-of-appeal-clarifie)). Jurisdiction-specific, severable clause; no US arbitration for Canadian consumers.
11. **Privacy** — PIPEDA (Canada), with Law 25 reserved for Quebec and federal Bill C-36 (tabled June 15, 2026, [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2026/06/canada-tables-bill-c36-the-protecting-privacy-and-consumer-data-act)) monitored; CCPA/CPRA if global revenue > $26.625M or 100,000+ California consumers ([Feroot](https://www.feroot.com/blog/ccpa-applicability-website-california-law/)); Colorado Privacy Act at 100,000 Colorado consumers or 25,000 + sale ([Baker Donelson](https://bakerdonelson.com/consumer-data-privacy-law-guide-colorado)); NY SHIELD reasonable safeguards and 30-day breach notice ([Crowell](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/new-york-enacts-the-shield-act)); Pennsylvania breach law only, HB 78 pending; Ohio and Missouri breach laws. Geolocation and ID documents are sensitive — minimise, encrypt, time-limit.
12. **Age verification / COPPA** — hard verification before first deposit; no minors, so no parental-consent flows.
13. **Tax cooperation** — W-9/W-8 (or Canadian equivalent) before withdrawal above thresholds; consent to reporting; backup-withholding notice.
14. **Developer/marketplace terms** (separate) — revenue share, certification of no outcome-affecting RNG except identical-seed content, audit rights.
15. **Changes, suspension, limitation of liability, governing law** (Ontario for Canadians; state-specific for US users) with mandatory-law carve-outs.

## 7. Design mandates for engineering

1. **No outcome-affecting RNG unless identical for all competitors.** Procedural content derives from a per-match seed shared by every entrant; seed and generator version are logged. No random power-ups, loot, critical hits or variable multipliers in cash play. (*Ross*; GotSkill; NY/MO material degree.)
2. **Prize distribution is a deterministic, pre-disclosed function of rank.** No mystery prizes, jackpots or bonus rounds. (GotSkill 2025; *Torch*; s. 74.06.)
3. **Rake, prize table, fee and format are shown before the fee is debited** and are immutable once entries open. (s. 74.06; FTC § 5; UDAP.)
4. **No cash-contest entry while location is unverified or outside the allow-list**; re-verify at each entry and at withdrawal; block VPN/proxy; log the geo decision with the entry. (UIGEA state-law trigger; AB 831/S5935A vendor model.)
5. **Per-jurisdiction minimum age** (ON 19, AB 18, BC 19, NS 19, US 18) enforced from the verified document before first deposit.
6. **KYC before first deposit or withdrawal**, plus sanctions/PEP screening; retain results per policy.
7. **Withdrawals only to the original funding source and matching legal name**; no peer-to-peer transfers; no third-party funding. (AML; MTL/PSP positioning.)
8. **Player funds segregated with the licensed partner in FBO/trust accounts**; daily ledger-to-partner reconciliation; no commingling with operating cash. (FinCEN/state MTL; RPAA safeguarding.)
9. **Escrow at match lock, atomic settlement at finalisation**; voided matches auto-refund escrow.
10. **Deposit, loss and time limits** with 24-hour cooling-off on increases and immediate decreases; "take a break" and self-exclusion blocking cash play across all games and new-account creation.
11. **No wallet expiry, dormancy fees or forfeiture**; dormancy workflow: notify at 12 months, attempt return to source, then unclaimed-property process by jurisdiction. (Ontario gift-card rules; US escheat.)
12. **Bot and multi-account controls**: device/IP/behavioural fingerprinting, server-authoritative state, replay storage for every cash match, human-review queue; **no house bots in cash play**, practice bots labelled. (*Skillz v. Papaya*; FTC § 5.)
13. **Five-year retention** of identity, transactions, geo decisions, results, replays, limit changes and self-exclusions, with legal hold. (31 CFR 1010.430; PCMLTFA.)
14. **Tax data capture**: W-9/W-8BEN before withdrawal; cumulative net winnings per calendar year; 1099-MISC data at $2,000 (2026); annual statements for Canadians; 24% backup-withholding support.
15. **Sales tax engine** on cosmetics and (if advised) platform fees by verified jurisdiction; HST/GST by province.
16. **Promotional credits ring-fenced and non-withdrawable**, with official rules and no-purchase alternatives where chance is involved.
17. **Versioned, logged consent events** (terms, rules, privacy, AML), with the Ontario internet-agreement copy emailed within 15 days of account creation.
18. **Privacy by design**: coarse geo decision stored rather than continuous tracks; encrypted ID documents; deletion schedules; data-subject request tooling (PIPEDA/CCPA/CPA).
19. **Per-jurisdiction, per-game feature switches** so "lawful with conditions" answers (e.g., Pennsylvania deferral, card-game exclusions) need no code change.
20. **Kill switch and evidence pack**: suspend cash play in any jurisdiction within minutes; export seed, inputs, replay, participants, geo decisions and ledger entries for any contest within a day.
