# Geolocation, KYC, AML and responsible-gaming operating model

**Status:** research memo, 2026-10-01. **Owner:** founder / compliance officer (to be named).
**Scope:** Gnarcadia (Ontario corporation) — real-money skill-based multiplayer PWA arcade launching in Ontario, Alberta, British Columbia, Nova Scotia, California, Colorado, Missouri, New York, Ohio and Pennsylvania. Year-one target 2k–5k active players. The platform exposes `GeoProvider` and `KycProvider` interfaces so any vendor below can be swapped in.

> **Disclaimer.** This document is research to inform counsel and vendor selection. It is not legal advice. Skill-gaming legality is determined jurisdiction by jurisdiction and game by game; the controls below are what processors, banks and (voluntarily) regulators expect of a well-run operator, not a substitute for the legal opinion that the payments strategy depends on. Prices are as reported in October 2026 and must be confirmed with vendors.

## 1. Geolocation

### 1.1 Why it matters more than it looks

Skill gaming is legal in most but not all US states and is legal in Canada only as a game of **pure** skill (Criminal Code s.206 treats any structural element of chance as a lottery; see [Chambers Gaming Law 2024: Canada](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/gaming-law-2024/canada)). Ontario's Superior Court held in August 2025 that the "GotSkill" machines were games of mixed chance and skill despite the manufacturer's changes, and the AGCO followed with sanctions ([CasinoBeats](https://casinobeats.com/2025/09/05/ontario-gotskill-skill-games-ruling-us-comparison/), [InterGame](https://www.intergameonline.com/igaming/ontario-operators-sanctioned-amid-skill-based-games-ruling)). Cash contests on established skill platforms are unavailable in a cluster of US states: Skillz's legal page lists Arkansas, Connecticut, Delaware, Louisiana and South Dakota as prohibited jurisdictions (plus Maine and Indiana for card games), while more conservative lists add Arizona, Iowa, Montana, South Carolina and Tennessee ([Skillz legal](https://www.skillz.com/legal/), [Skillz support: can I play from anywhere](https://support.skillz.com/hc/en-us/articles/29419512229780-Can-I-play-from-anywhere), [Atay Games state guide 2026](https://ataygames.com/blogs/is-skill-based-gaming-legal-in-your-state), [Ifrah Law: enforcement against skill games in outlier states](https://www.ifrahlaw.com/ifrah-on-igaming/gambling-on-skill-enforcement-of-gambling-laws-against-skill-games-in-outlier-states/)). The legal opinion, processor approvals and T&Cs all assume paid play is confined to the approved list; geolocation makes that assumption true.

The design principle: **free play is open everywhere; paid entry requires a passing location check at the moment of entry.**

### 1.2 Tiered approach

**Tier A (launch): layered IP + device signals, built in-house behind `GeoProvider`.**

1. **IP geolocation** from a commercial database or API. MaxMind GeoIP2 City is US$134/month (US$122.83 billed annually) for the downloadable database with unlimited local lookups, with a free GeoLite2 tier that is intentionally less accurate ([fitgap: MaxMind GeoIP pricing](https://us.fitgap.com/products/maxmind-geoip), [MaxMind: upgrade from GeoLite2](https://support.maxmind.com/hc/en-us/articles/4407625342875-Upgrade-from-GeoLite2)). ipinfo.io's Core plan is US$49/month for 150k requests and Max US$156/month for 250k, with privacy (VPN/proxy/Tor/relay) detection included on Core and above ([TrustRadius: ipinfo pricing](https://tr-web.dev.trustradius.com/products/ipinfo-io/pricing), [ipinfo plans](https://ipinfo.io/blog/ipinfo-support-levels-and-plans)). IPQualityScore starts around US$99/month and returns a fraud score, proxy/VPN/Tor/bot flags and 25+ data points per IP ([Capterra: IPQS](https://www.capterra.ie/reviews/1015872/ipqualityscore), [IPQS proxy detection docs](https://ipqs.com/documentation/proxy-detection/overview)). Use two providers and require agreement on the state/province.
2. **HTML5 Geolocation API** cross-check on the device. On mobile this is GPS/Wi-Fi/cell with typical accuracy of 5–15 metres outdoors; on desktop it is Wi-Fi or IP-derived and can be spoofed by browser extensions, which are not available on mobile browsers ([Store Locator Widgets: HTML5 geolocation accuracy](https://www.storelocatorwidgets.com/blogpost/20453/Everything_you_ever_wanted_to_know_about_HTML5_Geolocation_Accuracy), [IP2Location: HTML5 vs IP geolocation](https://blog.ip2location.com/?p=5498), [ExpressVPN: HTML5 geolocation spoofing](https://ExpressVPN.com/support/troubleshooting/html5-geolocation-spoofing/)). Reverse-geocode the coordinates to a state/province server-side.
3. **VPN / proxy / datacenter / Tor / residential-proxy detection** from the IP providers above, plus ASN type (hosting vs. ISP vs. mobile carrier).
4. **Consistency checks:** browser timezone vs. expected timezone for the claimed state; `navigator.language`/locale; device clock skew; WebRTC leak of a local IP in a different region (where available); mismatch between KYC address state and location state (allowed, but raises score); impossible travel between consecutive checks.
5. **Re-check on every paid match entry** and every deposit/withdrawal, not only at login; cache a passing result for no more than 15–30 minutes and invalidate on IP change.

**Tier B (first scale step, or when a processor insists): Xpoint or Locance/LocationSmart.** Both are gaming-specific compliance geolocation vendors that price below GeoComply. Industry reporting puts the category at roughly US$0.01–0.05 per check with monthly minimums anywhere from US$5,000 to US$50,000 depending on vendor and operator size; Xpoint "deliberately undercuts" GeoComply and targets DFS/sweepstakes/new-entrant profiles, and its Trust Mode reduces check volume by 20%+ on trusted residential Wi-Fi ([Tech Insider: GeoComply vs Xpoint vs LocationSmart](https://tech-insider.org/igt-geocomply-vs-xpoint-vs-locationsmart-for-us-gambling-ge-en-d181/), [Track360: sportsbook geolocation 2026](https://track360.io/blog/sportsbook-geolocation-compliance-geocomply-vs-alternatives-2026), [SBC Americas: Xpoint Trust Mode](https://sbcamericas.com/2024/04/23/xpoint-launches-new-trust-mode-product/), [iGB: Xpoint funding](https://igamingbusiness.com/company-news/xpoint-secures-new-growth-funding-supercharging-rmg-geolocation-innovation/)). Locance (LocationSmart) is the lowest-commitment option, with tiered per-API-call pricing and a listed starting price of US$695/month, and explicitly serves sweepstakes and fantasy gaming ([Software Advice: Locance pricing](https://www.softwareadvice.com/scm/locationsmart-profile/), [Locance platform](https://www.locance.com/geolocation-platform/)). Exact minimums are quote-based; ask both for a "sub-10k checks/month" tier.

**Tier C (regulated-market grade): GeoComply.** The incumbent with 80%+ share of US regulated iGaming; per-check reportedly US$0.01–0.05 with the high end for small operators, monthly minimums reported at US$5,000–50,000, plus integration and per-state activation fees ([Tech Insider](https://tech-insider.org/igt-geocomply-vs-xpoint-vs-locationsmart-for-us-gambling-ge-en-d169/), [Casino Reports: geolocation competition](https://www.casinoreports.com/geolocation-xpoint-strategy/)). Not justified at Gnarcadia's scale unless a bank or a state inquiry demands it.

### 1.3 Accuracy limitations to design around

- IP geolocation is reliable at country level and progressively less so below it. Vendor and independent figures for US region/state accuracy range from "90%+" to 80–90% and as low as 55–80% depending on dataset and ISP, with the worst results at state borders and where an ISP routes traffic through a hub in another state; mobile carrier IPs are frequently located at the carrier gateway rather than the handset ([ipgeolocation.io guide](https://ipgeolocation.io/guides/what-is-ip-geolocation-how-it-works), [Abstract API: how accurate is IP geolocation](https://www.abstractapi.com/guides/ip-geolocation/how-accurate-is-ip-geolocation), [IPThreat: margin of error](https://ipthreat.net/blog/ip-address-geolocation-accuracy-what-the-margin-of-error-actually-costs-your-security-operations-188), [APIVerve: the IP that broke our geofencing](https://blog.apiverve.com/post/the-ip-address-that-broke-our-geofencing/)). MaxMind itself describes GeoLite2 as less accurate than GeoIP2 and publishes city-level accuracy by country rather than guaranteeing state-level results.
- Residential proxies present a real consumer's IP in the right state and are hard to detect; VPNs move the exit point entirely ([Massive: residential proxy glossary](https://www.joinmassive.com/glossary/what-is-ip-geolocation)).
- Desktop HTML5 geolocation can be spoofed by extension; mobile GPS can be spoofed by developer options or rooted devices (detectable via mock-location flags in native apps, less so in a browser).

### 1.4 Decision rules (launch configuration)

| Rule | Action |
|---|---|
| IP state/province (both providers) and HTML5 state/province all agree and are on the approved list | **Allow** paid entry; cache 15 min |
| Any disagreement between IP state and HTML5 state | **Block** paid entry; show "we couldn't confirm your location" with retry; log |
| Either IP provider flags VPN, proxy, Tor, hosting/datacenter ASN, or IPQS fraud score ≥ 75 (tune after 30 days) | **Block** paid entry; soft-block free play only for Tor |
| HTML5 permission denied on a mobile device | **Block** paid entry; explain that location permission is required for cash play (as Skillz does) |
| HTML5 permission denied on desktop | **Allow** only if both IP providers agree, no privacy flags, timezone consistent, and the account has completed Tier 1 KYC with an address in the same state; otherwise block |
| Timezone or locale inconsistent with claimed state | Add to risk score; block if combined with any other weak signal |
| Impossible travel (>800 km between checks <1 h apart) | Block and queue for review |
| Location within ~10 km of a border between an approved and a non-approved jurisdiction and HTML5 accuracy radius > 5 km | Require a fresh high-accuracy fix (`enableHighAccuracy: true`) before allowing; if still ambiguous, block |
| KYC address jurisdiction differs from location jurisdiction | Allow if location is approved (travellers are fine) but flag for the AML monitoring rules |

**Granularity.** Canada is gated by province and the US by state; nothing in the launch list needs county- or city-level fencing (unlike some US sweepstakes or tribal-land rules). Treat Quebec as excluded until counsel reviews its contest rules and French-language obligations; treat all non-listed provinces, states and countries as excluded. Record the approved list as configuration with an effective date so the audit trail shows which list applied to any entry.

**Logging and retention.** Log every check: timestamp, account id, match/transaction id, IP, both IP-provider results and raw flags, HTML5 coordinates and accuracy radius (rounded to ~100 m after decision to minimize data), reverse-geocoded jurisdiction, device fingerprint id, decision, rule that fired. Retain for 5 years alongside transaction records (the AML retention standard below); purge precise coordinates after 12 months.

### 2.2 Tiered KYC program

| Tier | Trigger | Collect / verify | Allowed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tier 0** | Account creation | Email (verified), DOB attestation, acceptance of T&Cs, location check, device fingerprint | Free play only; no deposits; no prizes |
| **Tier 1** | First deposit | Legal name, DOB, residential address; government ID document + selfie liveness (or Canadian non-document bureau match for low amounts); age ≥ age of majority in the player's jurisdiction (18 in Alberta, Ontario (for skill contests; 19 for regulated gambling), 19 in BC and Nova Scotia; 18 in the US launch states, with counsel to confirm whether 21 should apply anywhere); sanctions/PEP screen; name match to payment instrument | Deposits up to **$500 lifetime** (suggested; set with counsel) and winnings credited to balance |
| **Tier 2** | First withdrawal, or cumulative deposits > **$2,000**, or any single prize ≥ **US$2,000**, or risk flag | Enhanced due diligence: re-verified address (bank statement or utility, or bank-account name match from the pay-by-bank vendor), source-of-funds questionnaire for cumulative deposits > $10,000, **SSN (US) or SIN (Canada)** collected for tax reporting when a prize or annual net winnings reach reporting thresholds, bank account in the same name for payouts, manual review of the account's play history | Withdrawals; higher deposit limits |

Why US$2,000: the One Big Beautiful Bill Act raised the Form 1099-MISC reporting threshold for prizes and awards (and W-2G and backup withholding) from $600 to $2,000 starting tax year 2026, indexed from 2027 ([NATP: IRS thresholds change in 2026](https://www.natptax.com/news-insights/blog/irs-reporting-thresholds-change-in-2026-for-gambling-income/), [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102ku4y/one-big-beautiful-bill-act-could-mean-more-valuable-prizes/), [CPA Practice Advisor](https://cpapracticeadvisor.com/2025/07/30/one-big-beautiful-bill-act-changes-1099-thresholds/165863/)). Collect a W-9 (or W-8BEN for non-US players who somehow win US-sourced prizes, which the geofence should prevent) at Tier 2 rather than at the moment of a large win. Canada does not generally tax windfall prize winnings in the hands of recreational players, but counsel and the accountant should confirm treatment for frequent winners and for the platform's own reporting.

### 2.3 Record-keeping and privacy

- **AML-style retention:** keep identity, transaction and geolocation records for at least **5 years** after the account closes or the transaction occurs; FINTRAC requires reporting entities to keep records 5 years and FinCEN's MSB rules are similar, and processors will ask for the same even where Gnarcadia is not itself a reporting entity ([FINTRAC record keeping](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/guidance-directives/recordkeeping-document/record/fin-eng), [FinCEN record keeping](https://fincen.gov/record-keeping)). Seven years is the safer figure for tax and corporate records in Canada.
- **PIPEDA** (and Alberta's and BC's PIPA, which are deemed substantially similar and apply to intra-provincial activity; Nova Scotia has no private-sector statute so PIPEDA applies) requires retention only as long as necessary for the identified purposes, a documented retention schedule, and breach-of-security-safeguards reporting to the OPC and affected individuals where there is a real risk of significant harm ([OPC: Principle 5](https://priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/p_principle/principles/p_use), [OPC: provincial laws substantially similar](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/legislation-related-to-pipeda/provincial-legislation-deemed-substantially-similar-to-pipeda/), [DataGuidance: Nova Scotia](https://www.dataguidance.com/jurisdiction/nova-scotia), [Alberta OIPC PIPA breach process](https://oipc.ab.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/PIPA-Privacy-Breach-Process-April-2024.pdf)).
- **CCPA/CPRA** applies once annual gross revenue exceeds $25.625 million (2025 adjustment) or the business handles personal information of 100,000+ California residents, or derives half its revenue from selling/sharing data ([CookieYes: who does CCPA apply to](https://www.cookieyes.com/blog/who-does-ccpa-apply-to/)). The **Colorado Privacy Act** has no revenue test and applies at 100,000 Colorado consumers (25,000 if data is sold) ([Baker Donelson: Colorado guide](https://bakerdonelson.com/consumer-data-privacy-law-guide-colorado)). Gnarcadia will be under these thresholds in year one but should build the rights workflows (access, deletion, opt-out) now because the data model is cheaper to get right early.
- **Data minimization:** do not store document images or selfies; do not store full coordinates after the decision; tokenize SSN/SIN with a KMS-backed vault and restrict access to the tax workflow; keep the fraud/device data in a separate store with its own retention.

## 3. AML program for a small operator

### 3.1 Is Gnarcadia a reporting entity?

Probably not, but the answer depends on the custody model chosen in [payments.md](./payments.md). In the US, a platform whose acceptance and transmission of funds is integral to its own service can fall outside FinCEN's money-transmitter definition; if it does not, it becomes an MSB with registration, a written AML program under 31 CFR 1022.210 (policies and controls, a compliance officer, training, independent review) and SAR filing obligations ([FinCEN MSB final rule](https://www.fincen.gov/sites/default/files/shared/MSB_Final_Rule_Definition_and_OtherRegulations.pdf), [private.law: FinCEN MSB registration](https://wiki.private.law/en/msb-fincen-usa)). In Canada, FINTRAC's MSB definition (money transferring, FX, virtual currency, money orders, crowdfunding platforms) does not obviously capture a skill-gaming operator settling its own entry fees and prizes, and the "casino" reporting-entity category is for licensed casinos; but FINTRAC's online-gambling bulletin tells the PSPs and banks that serve gambling-like sites what to look for, and those PSPs will push the monitoring onto Gnarcadia contractually ([FINTRAC bulletin](https://fintrac-canafe.canada.ca/intel/bulletins/gambling-jeu-eng.pdf), [BLG analysis](https://www.blg.com/en/insights/2024/02/know-when-to-fold-em-special-bulletin-on-money-laundering-through-online-gambling-sites)). If counsel concludes Gnarcadia is an MSB in either country, the program below is already structured in the five FINTRAC elements and four FinCEN pillars, with a two-year effectiveness review in Canada ([Substance Law: FINTRAC compliance program](https://substancelaw.ca/compliance-program/), [Alessa: five pillars](https://alessa.com/blog/five-pillars-of-bsa-aml-compliance-program/)).

### 3.2 The voluntary program processors expect regardless

1. **Risk assessment** (written, reviewed annually): products (entry fees, prizes, balances), customers (age bands, jurisdictions, high-rollers), channels (PWA only; no agents), geography (10 jurisdictions; cross-border between Canada and the US is not permitted within a match pool unless counsel approves), payment methods (card and ACH reversible; Interac irrevocable).
2. **Compliance officer:** a named individual (the founder at launch, with a plan to move it to a part-time fractional compliance officer within 12 months) with authority to freeze accounts and payouts.
3. **Policies and procedures:** KYC tiers above; sanctions screening at onboarding and daily re-screen of the active base against OFAC SDN and Canada's Consolidated Canadian Autonomous Sanctions List (SEMA and JVCFOA) plus UN lists ([OpenSanctions: Canadian consolidated list](https://opensanctions.org/datasets/ca_dfatd_sema_sanctions), [Miller Thomson: are you sanctions compliant](https://www.millerthomson.com/en/insights/global-trade-customs/are-you-sanctions-compliant-what-every-business-must-know-part-1-4/)); transaction limits; escalation; record retention; a decision log for every manual override.
4. **Training:** annual, documented, for everyone who touches support, payouts or game review; a 20-minute onboarding module is enough at this size.
5. **Independent review:** every two years at minimum, by an outside AML consultant or counsel; keep the report for the processors.
6. **Suspicious-activity monitoring** (automated rules on the ledger and match data, with a weekly review queue):
   - **Structuring:** multiple deposits just under a Tier threshold or under $1,000/$3,000 in a short window; deposits from several instruments to one account.
   - **Rapid deposit–withdraw cycles:** deposit followed by withdrawal within 24–72 hours with minimal play (the deposit-to-play rule blocks most of this; log attempts).
   - **Chip dumping / collusion:** repeated head-to-head matches between the same two accounts where one consistently loses, especially with scores far below that account's historical skill; accounts that only play each other; shared devices or IPs between opponents; funds flowing one direction across accounts over time.
   - **Multi-accounting:** shared device fingerprint, payment instrument, bank account, address, phone or selfie across accounts; sequential sign-ups from one IP; Fingerprint's Pro Plus tier is US$99/month for 20,000 identifications with a 1,000-call free tier ([c/side: Fingerprint pricing](https://cside.com/blog/fingerprintjs-pricing)).
   - **Bonus abuse:** promotional credit cashed out with minimal play; referral chains that resolve to one person.
   - **Third-party funding:** name mismatch between the payment instrument and the KYC record; refuse rather than monitor.
   - **Sanctions / PEP hits** and adverse media on high-rollers.
   - **Geolocation anomalies** feeding the AML queue: impossible travel, VPN attempts after a block, location state different from KYC state for a majority of sessions.
7. **Reporting:** if deemed an MSB, file SARs (FinCEN) or STRs (FINTRAC) on the statutory timelines and keep supporting material 5 years; if not, document the decision to freeze/close and, where the processor's agreement requires it, notify the processor. Keep a single case-management log (a ticketing project is adequate) so the independent reviewer can sample it.

## 4. Responsible gaming and consumer protection

Skill contests are not gambling in law, but they are gambling-adjacent in the eyes of banks, processors and players' families. Ontario's **Registrar's Standards for Internet Gaming** are the most detailed North American benchmark and are explicitly cited by AGCO when it fines operators (for example a CA$100,000 penalty for responsible-gambling failures in 2023); they require an easy and obvious way to set financial and time limits at registration and any time after, a well-promoted self-exclusion mechanism, policies to identify and minimize harm, tailored interventions, and trained staff ([AGCO Registrar's Standards for Internet Gaming](https://www.agco.ca/node/81021), [AGCO penalty bulletin](https://www.agco.ca/en/news/agco-issues-100000-penalties-apollo-entertainment-violations-internet-gaming-responsible)). Adopting them voluntarily is cheap insurance and a strong underwriting talking point. Controls to implement at launch:

- **Deposit limits:** player-set daily, weekly and monthly limits, with a default (e.g. $200/day, $500/week, $1,500/month for new accounts); decreases take effect immediately, increases after a 24-hour (daily) or 7-day (weekly/monthly) cooling period.
- **Entry-fee / loss limits:** a separate cap on entry fees per day and net loss per week; a soft stop with a reality-check message when 80% is reached.
- **Session reminders:** on-screen reality checks every 60 minutes of paid play showing time played, entry fees paid and net result; optional session-length limit.
- **Cooling-off (break in play):** 24 hours, 72 hours, 7 days, 30 days, self-selected; paid play and deposits disabled; no marketing.
- **Self-exclusion:** 6 months, 1 year, 5 years, indefinite; account frozen, balance returned to the player's verified source, marketing suppressed, re-registration blocked using hashed identity attributes and device signals. Ontario launched a centralized self-exclusion program in which registered iGaming operators must participate and must prevent centrally self-excluded persons from opening or accessing accounts ([AGCO CSE bulletin](https://www.agco.ca/en/news/information-bulletin-agco-sets-igaming-standards-centralized-self-exclusion-program), [Canadian Gaming Business: Ontario self-exclusion 2026](https://www.canadiangamingbusiness.com/2025/12/29/ontario-igaming-self-exclusion-2026/)). Gnarcadia is not a registered operator and will not have access to the provincial lists (Ontario's CSE, BC's GameSense/BCLC program, AGLC's and NS's programs); the honest position is: operate an internal list, honour any player's written request to be excluded immediately, and ask counsel whether voluntary data-sharing with any provincial program is possible.
- **Under-age controls:** DOB at Tier 0, document age check at Tier 1 before any deposit, no cash play for anyone below the age of majority in their jurisdiction, parental-request takedown process, no marketing in youth-oriented channels, and a design review of game art and copy against "appeals to minors" criteria.
- **Pre-entry disclosure:** before confirming any paid entry show the entry fee, the number of players, the total prize pool, the platform fee as an amount and a percentage, the payout structure, and the player's current balance; store the exact disclosure shown with the match record.
- **Account statements:** downloadable history of deposits, entry fees, prizes and withdrawals, with a "net position" figure on the wallet screen.
- **Dispute and complaint process:** in-product ticket with reference number; first response within 2 business days; written outcome within 10; escalation to a named manager; the published disconnect/refund policy (section 5); an annual complaint summary kept for processors.
- **Help resources:** ConnexOntario, GameSense (BC), the Alberta and Nova Scotia helplines and the US National Problem Gambling Helpline linked from the wallet and limits pages.

## 5. Fair play, anti-cheat and integrity

A skill-gaming platform's legal position rests on the proposition that the better player wins. That is a technical claim as much as a legal one, and the opinion letter will ask how it is enforced.

- **Server-authoritative scoring.** The client renders; the server owns the game state, validates every input against the rules and computes the score. Where a game genuinely cannot run server-side, run a deterministic simulation on the server from the recorded input stream and reject any client score that does not reproduce.
- **Deterministic seeds.** Any pseudo-random content (level layout, piece order, spawn pattern) is generated from a per-match seed that is identical for all players in the match and published after the match, so that no player can gain a chance advantage; this is also what makes the "pure skill" argument credible in Canada and the "chance does not materially affect outcome" argument in US predominance states. Seeds are committed (hash published) before the match and revealed after.
- **Input and replay logging.** Record timestamped inputs and periodic state checksums for every paid match; keep for 90 days (longer if disputed). Replays support both dispute resolution and training of anomaly models.
- **Anomaly detection.** Flag superhuman reaction times, machine-regular input patterns, scores far outside a player's rolling distribution, sudden skill jumps and identical input sequences across accounts; start with z-scores and rules.
- **Bot detection.** Browser integrity signals (headless flags, WebDriver), input entropy, pointer-movement curvature and device fingerprinting; flagged accounts get an interactive challenge before paid play.
- **Collusion and match-fixing.** The AML rules above (repeat pairings, one-directional value flow, shared devices) plus server-side matchmaking and bracket seeding so players cannot pick their cash-match opponent.
- **Device fingerprinting.** Used for multi-accounting, self-exclusion enforcement and bot detection; disclosed in the privacy policy; retained with the fraud data.
- **Game-upload review.** Each indie-developer game passes a checklist before it can host cash matches: deterministic given the seed, server-verifiable score, no hidden state that advantages one player, no pay-to-win elements, no mechanics that resolve by chance, published rules, and a skill-evidence test (a panel of experienced players should beat novices with a consistent, statistically significant margin across repeated runs; keep the results for the opinion letter). Games fail closed: cash matches are disabled until review passes, and any post-launch change to game logic re-triggers review.
- **Disconnects and refunds (published policy).** If a match cannot be completed because of a platform-side failure, all entry fees are refunded and the platform fee is waived. If one player disconnects, a short reconnection window applies (e.g. 60 seconds); after that the match is scored on completed play if the game design supports it, otherwise the disconnected player forfeits. Repeated "convenient" disconnects by one account are an anomaly signal. Players can dispute within 48 hours with the match id; the replay is the evidence; decisions are logged.

## 6. Security and liability checklist

- **PCI DSS.** Use only hosted fields or redirects so Gnarcadia qualifies for SAQ A. Under PCI DSS v4.0.1 (effective 1 April 2025 for these items) an SAQ A merchant embedding a processor's iframe must either implement Requirements 6.4.3 (authorize, inventory and integrity-check every script on the payment page) and 11.6.1 (detect unauthorized changes to payment-page headers and scripts) or obtain written confirmation from the processor that its solution protects against script attacks ([PCI SSC: FAQ on SAQ A eligibility](https://blog.pcisecuritystandards.org/faq-clarifies-new-saq-a-eligibility-criteria-for-e-commerce-merchants), [Report URI: SAQ A plain-English FAQ](https://blog.report-uri.com/pci-dss-4-0-1-saq-a-and-client-side-security-a-plain-english-faq/)). A strict Content Security Policy with script allow-listing and subresource integrity on the cashier page meets the spirit of both.
- **"SOC 2-lite" controls** (document them even without an audit, because processors ask): SSO/MFA on every admin and cloud console; least-privilege IAM with quarterly access reviews; encrypted data at rest and in transit; secrets in a managed vault; immutable audit logs for ledger changes and admin actions; change management through pull requests with a second reviewer for anything touching money; dependency and container scanning; daily encrypted backups with a tested restore; environment separation; vendor risk register (every vendor in this document, with their SOC 2 / ISO status).
- **Incident response.** A written plan with roles, severity levels, a 1-hour internal escalation target for suspected breach of personal or payment data, evidence preservation, processor and bank notification steps, and the breach-notification matrix below. Tabletop it twice a year.
- **Breach-notification laws in the launch jurisdictions** (confirm with counsel; several changed recently): PIPEDA, Alberta PIPA and (public-sector only) BC require notification where there is a real risk of significant harm, to the regulator and individuals, "as soon as feasible" / "without unreasonable delay"; **California** requires consumer notice within 30 days under a 2026 amendment; **Colorado** 30 days; **New York** 30 days from discovery, with NYDFS added to the regulators to notify, under the December 2024 amendment; **Ohio** 45 days; **Pennsylvania** "without unreasonable delay" with 2023 amendments expanding covered data to online credentials and medical data; **Missouri** "without unreasonable delay", with AG notice if 1,000 or more residents are notified ([PrivacyLawMap: state breach laws 2026](https://privacylawmap.com/blog/data-breach-notification-laws-by-state-2026), [Inside Privacy: New York amendment](https://www.insideprivacy.com/cybersecurity-2/new-york-adopts-amendment-to-the-state-data-breach-notification-law/), [Buchanan Ingersoll: Pennsylvania amendments](https://www.bipc.com/pennsylvania-amends-its-breach-notification-law), [DWT: Missouri](https://www.dwt.com/gcp/states/missouri), [Gowling WLG: Canada breach notification overview](https://gowlingwlg.com/getmedia/51ecbd41-db33-43c3-afe4-a61cf0517dee/Gowling-WLG-2023-Canada-Privacy-Breach-Notification-Overview.pdf.xml)). Processors' agreements usually require notice to them within 24–72 hours of a suspected cardholder-data incident.
- **Insurance.** Cyber liability is roughly US$1,000–1,600/year for a generic US$1M small-business policy and US$1,800–5,000/year for early-stage tech companies holding customer data; MFA is the single largest premium lever ([Secureframe: cyber insurance costs](https://secureframe.com/blog/cyber-insurance-costs), [QuoteSweep 2026](https://www.quotesweep.com/blog/cyber-insurance-cost)). Real-money gaming is a rated class, so expect the higher end and some exclusions; buy cyber plus technology E&O (contract and performance claims from players and developers), general liability, and directors' and officers' cover before taking the first deposit. Media/IP liability for the uploaded-games marketplace should be included in the E&O form.
- **Indie-developer terms.** The developer agreement should include: representations of ownership and non-infringement; indemnity for IP, privacy and consumer claims arising from the game; a licence to Gnarcadia to host, modify for integrity purposes and remove; compliance with the game-review checklist and the prohibition on chance mechanics; revenue share and reporting; audit rights over game logic; a security requirement (no external network calls, no data collection beyond the SDK); and termination for cheating or legal risk.
- **DMCA / IP review of uploads.** Register a DMCA agent with the US Copyright Office and publish a notice-and-takedown process (Canada's notice-and-notice regime differs; publish both); pre-publication review for obvious clones of well-known titles, trademarked characters, licensed music and sprite rips; a repeat-infringer policy; and takedown tooling that disables cash matches immediately while the dispute is handled.
- **Content rating.** Adopt an internal rating (e.g. ESRB/IARC-style descriptors) for every game, gate anything above "Teen" descriptors behind the age-verified tier, and keep the marketplace free of real-money games with gambling imagery (slot reels, cards, dice) that would undercut the skill position and trip the processors' brand-risk reviews.
- **Terms of service points:** governing law and venue; arbitration/class waiver only where enforceable (not against Ontario consumers under the Consumer Protection Act); eligibility by jurisdiction and age; fee and payout disclosure; the disconnect/refund policy; dormant-balance handling (return funds; US unclaimed-property rules apply); and the right to void matches for integrity reasons.
